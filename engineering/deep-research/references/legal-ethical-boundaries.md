# Legal and ethical research boundaries

This skill is for **public-source research**. It is not a license to bypass access controls, scrape in violation of terms, or collect personal data “because it might be useful”.

## Allowed

- Public web pages, official gazettes, court databases that permit public access, open data portals, preprints, published papers, company IR pages, public social posts treated as *testimony*.
- User-supplied documents they have a right to share.
- Summarizing publicly filed litigation and legislation.
- Marking paywalled claims as unverified and seeking an open primary (author manuscript, DOI, official mirror, Hansard, gazette).

## Not allowed

- Circumventing paywalls, logins, CAPTCHAs, or robots rules to get a full text.
- Credential stuffing, password guessing, or using leaked datasets of personal information.
- Doxxing, stalking, or assembling dossiers on private individuals beyond what the user already provided and needs for a legitimate case they own.
- Treating private Slack/email dumps found online as fair game without legal basis.
- Medical/legal **advice** masquerading as research. Research can map the rule; it cannot tell a person what they should file tomorrow as counsel.

## PII and sensitive topics

- Minimize: do not copy extra identifiers into the evidence store.
- Redact names of non-public people in exported memos unless the user is the data subject or counsel of record.
- Trauma-informed tone for violence, FDV, disability, and self-representation contexts: precise, not graphic, not paternal.
- If the query appears designed to harm, refuse the harmful slice and keep any legitimate public-policy remainder.

## Terms of service and rate

- Prefer official APIs or user-owned tools when bulk collection is needed.
- Do not write exploit-like scrapers. Query expansion is for humans/agents to type into search tools they already have.
- Archive links (Wayback) are fine for *public* snapshots; still attribute the original.

## Conflicts and independence

Disclose when a source is the user’s employer, opponent, or funder. Down-weight; do not hide.

## Output ethics

- No fabricated citations, quotes, or case names. If a tool failed, say so.
- No silent interpolation of statute text. Quote or paraphrase with a locator.
- Date-stamp volatile topics.
- Steelman conspiracy or polarized claims, then apply the rubric — do not moralize in place of evidence.

## Jurisdictional research (esp. employment / AU)

You may map Fair Work, general protections, reverse onus, procedural fairness **as research**. You may not claim to be the user’s lawyer. Point to dedicated legal skills only as optional companions if they exist in the user’s library.
